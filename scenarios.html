The Economics Endgame: Scenarios to mid-2031

The predictions page deliberately refuses to call bubble dynamics, China–US competition, or company outcomes — the variance swamps the signal for trend extrapolation. This page makes exactly those calls anyway, using a different tool: explicit scenarios with probabilities, derived from an economic model of the AI buildout rather than from trend lines. Expect these numbers to be wrong; the point is to be wrong in a way we can measure and update.

The core thesis this page tests. The US is making a GDP-scale capital bet (~$710–725B Big-Four capex in 2026, ~$7.6T projected through 2031) that AI productivity gains will be monetizable enough to cover a ~$1T/yr profit requirement, on assets that depreciate in 3–5 years. Our analysis says the dominant failure mode is not "AI doesn't work" — it's "AI works and becomes too cheap to meter." Every capability level commoditizes on a 3–6 month open-weights fuse (1–2 months in coding); China has industrialized that fuse as national strategy; and edge inference threatens to extend commoditization to the serving layer. In most branches below, the technology succeeds while much of the capital that built it earns commodity returns. The railway pattern.

02

Five scenarios, weighted

Mutually exclusive, jointly exhaustive to a first approximation, resolved as of mid-2031. Weights are subjective probabilities conditioned on the July 2026 data in the factual report. A correction followed by a commoditized recovery counts as C, not B — C is "the adjustment happens violently," B is "it happens gradually."

Scenario weights, July 2026
Our subjective distribution. The single most likely world is a violent correction — but "no crash" branches (A + B) sum to 50%, so this is not a doom call.
SCENARIO A

Frontier Rents Hold

The labs' bull case: long-horizon agency resists distillation, and the lag widens.

20%weight

The one capability gap open weights have not closed — long-horizon agentic reliability (10–25 pts on SWE-bench Pro / Terminal-Bench / OSWorld class benchmarks) — turns out to be structurally hard to imitate, because it comes from expensive RL against real environments rather than from distillable outputs. Frontier training runs pass $10B+, the open-weights lag widens to 12+ months on the workloads that matter economically (multi-day autonomous work), and the endogenous task frontier keeps generating a monetizable edge. The labs' rent window reopens each generation; IPO valuations are broadly vindicated; capex is absorbed by genuine agentic demand. Government gating (Glasswing, Daybreak, the Fable 5 export-control suspension) hardens into a de facto regulatory moat around the closed frontier.

Who wins

Frontier labs, Nvidia, hyperscalers — the stack as priced today. Users still gain, but the builders keep an unusually large surplus share by historical GPT-buildout standards.

Early signatures

Agentic-benchmark gap stable or widening through 2027; open-weights threshold-lag on SWE Marathon-class tasks >12 mo; lab gross margins improving; China's open releases stall below frontier agency.

What kills it

One Chinese or Meta open release matching frontier long-horizon agency within 6 months of the closed SOTA. That single observation moves most of this weight to B/C.

SCENARIO B

Commoditized Boom (soft landing)

AI succeeds; margins die quietly; nobody gets a crash to point at.

30%weight

The conveyor keeps running at 3–6 months, agentic gaps close on schedule (the coding gap already went from ~15 months to 1–2), and token prices keep collapsing — but demand grows fast enough (Jevons, agent swarms, background workloads) that datacenters stay full and hyperscaler revenue keeps growing. What compresses is margins and multiples, gradually: inference reprices toward electricity-plus-amortization, the model layer pivots to applications and distribution (already visible: labs selling coding agents and outcomes, Meta selling API access), and capex growth bends to 15–20%/yr by 2028 without a discrete shock. Equity holders experience five years of de-rating rather than a crash; the productivity surplus diffuses to users. By 2031 "AI infrastructure" trades like a utility-industrial hybrid, and the 2026 vintage of lab valuations looks like the 1999 vintage of telecom valuations — wrong, but unwound in slow motion.

Who wins

End users, application businesses with distribution, power/land/HBM owners, device silicon (edge inference). Hyperscalers survive on volume. Labs survive as product companies, not model companies.

Early signatures

Capital intensity declining by 2H'27; token price index falling >5×/yr while aggregate token volume grows faster; lab revenue mix shifting >50% to apps/seats; no single-quarter capex cut >20%.

What kills it

Debt and circularity: if the unwind runs through credit rather than equity multiples, B becomes C. The $108B/yr+ hyperscaler debt issuance and lab compute-commitment collateral chains are the fragility.

SCENARIO C

The Correction

Same destination as B, reached violently. The single most likely world.

30%weight

A trigger — a Chinese open release matching or beating SOTA, a blown lab earnings print post-IPO, a hyperscaler demand-miss, or simply the 2027 capex guidance failing to justify itself — flips the narrative from Jevons to stranded assets faster than markets can reprice gradually. The transmission chain (§3) runs: lab equity collapses, the compute commitments backing Oracle/CoreWeave/Nvidia backlogs are doubted, circular vendor financing and SPV debt unwind, capex guidance is slashed, and because AI capex had become a meaningful share of measured GDP growth, the macro data rolls over too. An "AI winter" of 18–36 months in capital formation — not in usage, which grows throughout (the fiber precedent). Distressed datacenter capacity is repriced to cents on the dollar, and the 2029–2031 recovery is built on it by whoever kept distribution and balance sheets — likely including some of today's losers' assets under new owners. Technology outcome: identical to B. Financial outcome: wealth transfer from the 2024–2027 capital vintage to everyone who buys the wreckage.

Who wins

Late buyers of distressed capacity; users (uninterrupted); China strategically (asymmetric exposure: state-funded buildout doesn't mark to market daily; US concentration in equity indices is historic).

Early signatures

Credit spreads on datacenter/neocloud debt widening before equity moves; a second DeepSeek-moment single-day shock; insider selling at newly public labs; a top-3 lab cutting compute commitments.

What kills it

Either A (rents hold, so no trigger lands) or a genuinely smooth deleveraging (B). The Jan 2025 DeepSeek shock — $600B off Nvidia in a day, then full recovery — shows the market can absorb one narrative hit. C requires the hit to land on the credit layer, which grows more loaded each quarter.

SCENARIO D

Plateau Bust

Capability progress stalls; the lag closes to zero; everything reprices at once.

10%weight

The escalator stops: the benchmark-saturation machine (every 2024–2025 benchmark partially solved within ~12 months) finally hits a wall on long-horizon autonomy, and 2027–2028 frontier runs deliver marginal gains at 10× cost. With no moving edge to monetize, the open-weights lag closes automatically — matching a frozen frontier is indistinguishable from beating it, and China doesn't even need a breakthrough. This is the only branch that's bearish for infrastructure volume, not just margins: training demand (the biggest single driver of frontier capex) evaporates, replacement-cycle expectations collapse, and Nvidia's multiple — a claim on perpetually growing high-margin replacement demand — is hit hardest of all. Note the irony our analysis kept finding: this is a world where the technology disappoints and yet inference of already-achieved capability keeps diffusing profitably at commodity prices. Deployment continues; the frontier bet dies.

Who wins

Application layer and users (capability already banked is enormous — 2026-frontier agents are transformative even frozen). Chinese stack relatively (cheapest server of a frozen frontier wins on cost).

Early signatures

Watch the predictions page's Pred. 01 break-clause: a frontier lab failing to scale past ~10²⁷ FLOP for 18+ months despite capital; SWE Marathon-class scores flat across two model generations; labs stretching release cadences.

What kills it

The entire 2020–2026 record: no capability plateau has lasted 18 months yet, and mid-2026 (FrontierMath Tier 4 falling, multi-agent test-time compute) shows no deceleration. Hence only 10%.

SCENARIO E

Tail Events

The branches where this page's framework itself stops applying.

10%weight

Grouped residual: (i) a Taiwan/TSMC disruption, which resets every chip-linked number on this site to "unknown" and dominates all other dynamics; (ii) a genuine fast-takeoff/recursive-improvement outcome where frontier capability compounds so fast that economic categories (rents, commoditization lags) stop being the right lens — note the 2026 precedent of government-gated releases and export-controlled models suggests the state, not the market, would set the terms in that world; (iii) a hard regulatory severing of open weights (US criminalization of frontier-scale weight publication plus allied enforcement), which would manufacture Scenario A by law rather than by technology. Each individually ≤5%; jointly capped at 10% for this page.

Who wins

Undefined — that's what makes them tails. (iii) is the one lab equity quietly prices as upside.

Early signatures

(i) blockade/exercise escalation; (ii) a model materially accelerating its own successor's development, disclosed or leaked; (iii) AI OVERWATCH-style bills extending from chips to weights.

What kills it

Nothing kills a tail; it just fails to materialize. Reassess weights quarterly.

03

The transmission chain (how C actually happens)

The mechanism that separates a narrative shock (survivable — see January 2025) from a financial event. The load path runs through commitments and credit, not sentiment. Steps 1–6 are the cascade; steps 7–8 are the circuit breakers that have to fail first.

1
Trigger lands on the rent story
An open-weights release matches/beats the closed frontier on economically-relevant agentic work, or a newly-public lab posts numbers that make the $3T revenue path unbelievable. Key asymmetry: the Jevons counter-narrative rescues infrastructure but cannot rescue the labs — a rent-story hit has no natural buyer of the dip at the model layer.
2
Lab equity reprices
OpenAI and Anthropic are public (both filed June 2026): the repricing hits indices, retail, and — critically — the perceived creditworthiness of their forward compute commitments.
3
Backlogs are doubted
Those commitments are the collateral behind Oracle's 4.5 GW OpenAI agreement, CoreWeave's $87.8B backlog, and a large slice of Nvidia's order book. Analysts start applying haircuts; counterparty risk becomes the story.
4
Circular financing unwinds
Vendor equity stakes in customers who buy the vendor's chips, SPV-financed campuses, and $108B+/yr of hyperscaler debt issuance all mark against falling collateral simultaneously. This is where 2026 differs most from 2000: the leverage is in the chain, not just the valuations.
5
Capex guidance breaks
Two or more of MSFT/GOOG/META/AMZN cut guidance in the same quarter — the predictions page's own break-clause for its most locked-in physical projection. Neoclouds and single-tenant campuses (the marginal, debt-financed capacity) go first.
6
Macro print
AI capex had been contributing a meaningful share of measured US GDP growth — the boom was partly measuring spending on itself. The unwind subtracts it symmetrically, converting a sector event into a macro event, with the equity market at historic concentration in exactly these names.
B1
Circuit breaker: hyperscaler balance sheets
Unlike 2001's telecoms, the Big Four fund most capex from operating cash flow of monopoly-adjacent core businesses. They can absorb enormous writedowns without distress. The cascade needs the neocloud/SPV/lab periphery to be big enough to matter — it grows every quarter, which is why C's weight rises over time.
B2
Circuit breaker: demand response
Every price collapse so far has been met by faster volume growth within quarters. If distressed capacity reprices tokens down 10× and usage responds like 2023–2026 usage did, the trough is shallow and short. Fiber's problem was that 2002 demand couldn't absorb 2000 supply for years; agents may absorb it in months.

Thirteen predictions with probabilities

Concrete, resolvable, dated. These are consistency-checked against the scenario weights (e.g. P09 ≈ P(C) + P(D) + part of E). Resolution criteria are written so a future reader can score them without arguing definitions.

P01 · resolves 2027-12-3170%yes

BasisPrecedent set January 2025 (~$600B, Nvidia). Concentration is higher now, the release cadence is faster (14 frontier-relevant releases in H1 2026 alone), and two labs are newly public — more surface area for the same shock.

P02 · resolves 2027-12-3170%yes

OpenAI and Anthropic both complete IPOs, at a combined initial market cap above $1.5T

Resolves YES if both list and day-one combined cap ≥$1.5T (Anthropic's last private mark alone was $965B, May 2026).

BasisBoth filed in June 2026. Monetizing the promise while the rent window is open is the rational move given claims 1–3, and the window for a hot listing narrows in scenarios B/C — they know it. Residual 30% covers market-condition delays and a pre-listing shock.

P03 · resolves 2027-12-3160%yes

The capex bend becomes visible: Big-Four 2027 aggregate capex grows <40% YoY (vs +77% in 2026)

Resolves YES if combined MSFT/GOOG/META/AMZN 2027 capex lands below ~$1.0T (i.e. below 1.40 × the ~$710B 2026 actual/guide).

BasisCapital intensity at 45–57% of revenue, negative FCF at several majors, $108B/yr debt issuance — the predictions page's Pred. 04 mechanism. Analysts project $1T+; we take the under, mildly. A 2026-H2 shock (P01) accelerates this; scenario A delays it.

P04 · resolves 2027-12-3160%yes

The open-weights agentic gap closes below 6 months

Resolves YES if the best open-weights model reaches within 5 points (or 6-month threshold-lag) of the closed SOTA on the then-current agentic frontier benchmarks (SWE-bench Pro / Terminal-Bench / OSWorld class), sustained for a quarter.

BasisThe coding gap went ~15 → 1–2 months in eighteen months; agentic RL environments are being commoditized (open agent-swarm stacks, K2.6's 300-agent swarms); DeepSeek V4-Pro is already at 69% SWE-bench Verified vs 78% closed. This is the single most scenario-discriminating observable: YES moves weight from A to B/C; NO strengthens A.

P06 · resolves 2028-12-3165%yes

BasisClaim 3: token rents have a months-long half-life, application/distribution moats don't. The pivot is already visible (coding agents, enterprise workflow products, Meta's bundling). Verticalization is the only escape route the labs fully control.

P07 · resolves 2028-12-3150%yes

Resolves YES if a Chinese-lab open-weights model tops a recognized agentic/coding leaderboard (SWE-bench Pro standardized, Terminal-Bench, AA Coding Agent Index) ahead of all closed models for a continuous month.

BasisThe full state apparatus is behind this (NDRC plan, National AI Fund equity in DeepSeek, Ascend vertical integration) — but leading (not following) requires frontier-scale exploration compute that export controls still constrain. Coin-flip is honest. YES is the strongest single trigger for scenario C.

P08 · resolves 2028-12-3145%yes

An AI financial event by end-2028

Resolves YES if an AI equity basket (NVDA + top neocloud + public labs) draws down ≥30% from peak within ≤6 months and AI-linked credit spreads (datacenter ABS, neocloud debt) widen ≥300bps in the same window.

BasisThe two-year slice of P09. Requires trigger + credit transmission (§3) landing early. The debt/circularity stock is still building, which is why the 5-year number (55%) is higher than 2×-ish this one implies.

By mid-2031 (full horizon)

P09 · resolves 2031-07-1755%yes

An AI financial event at some point in the five years

Resolves YES under P08's criteria at any point before mid-2031. This is the page's headline number: P(C) + P(D) + ~half of E, minus a sliver for definitional near-misses.

BasisCapex–revenue divergence already past the 2001 telecom peak; assets on a 3–5 year depreciation clock; commoditization conveyor state-funded; leverage migrating from equity to credit each quarter. Against: hyperscaler cash-flow ballast and the demand response (circuit breakers B1/B2), which is why this isn't 75%.

P10 · resolves 2031-07-1790%yes

Aggregate inference demand grows every single year, through any correction

Resolves YES if global AI inference compute consumption (tokens or FLOP-equivalents, best-available estimate) is higher each calendar year 2026→2030, including any crash years.

BasisClaim 5 (Jevons) plus the strongest precedent in the dataset: fiber traffic grew through the entire telecom bust. Inference prices fell ~1,000× in 3 years while usage exploded. Only scenario E(i) plausibly breaks this. Our highest-confidence call.

P11 · resolves 2031-07-1760%yes

Inference reprices to utility economics

Resolves YES if frontier-quality tokens (top-decile capability of the day) fall ≥100× in price from July 2026 ($5/$30 per Mtok, GPT-5.6) and major-cloud AI inference gross margins are credibly reported below 30%.

BasisOpen weights already serve near-frontier at $0.12/Mtok (~42× under GPT-5.6 input pricing) — most of the 100× exists today across the quality gap; the question is whether frontier quality itself converges onto commodity serving. B and C both imply YES; A implies NO for the frontier tier.

P12 · resolves 2031-07-1785%yes

A 2026-frontier-equivalent model runs locally on flagship phones — and datacenter demand grows anyway

Resolves YES if a locally-running phone model matches GPT-5.6/Fable-5-class scores on the 2026 benchmark suite (MMLU-Pro, GPQA, SWE-bench Verified) and P10 is still on track.

BasisDistillation trend (Qwen 4 4B already "best 8GB-tier"; 2023's frontier runs on 2026 laptops), NPU roadmaps, and every prior edge migration (mainframe→PC→phone) growing the datacenter alongside. Edge absorbs interactive work; long-horizon parallel agent work — where demand is heading — stays centralized on energy economics.

P13 · resolves 2031-07-1740%yes

The productivity payoff shows up in national statistics

Resolves YES if US nonfarm labor productivity growth ≥2.5%/yr for two consecutive calendar years before 2031 (vs ~1.5% post-2005 trend).

BasisThe entire bet's ultimate justification — and the hardest to time. Electricity took ~40 years to show up in productivity stats; the Solow paradox ("computers everywhere except the productivity statistics") lasted two decades. AI diffusion is faster than either, but organizational reorganization is the binding constraint, not capability. NO here does not mean the bet failed — it means the surplus is still in transit as of 2031. Deliberately our most humble number.

06

How this page relates to the predictions page

No contradiction, different tools. The predictions page extrapolates physical trends with high confidence and abstains where variance swamps signal. This page covers exactly the abstentions — bubble dynamics, China, company-layer outcomes — using scenario weights instead of point forecasts, at honestly lower confidence. Where the two overlap they agree: its Pred. 04 (capex bend) is our P03; its Pred. 08 (open-weight lag persists at 6–12 months) is the complement of our P04, and the two pages' numbers are consistent (they give the lag persisting; we give 60% that the agentic lag specifically compresses below 6 months — the same underlying distribution read from opposite sides). Its physical projections (compute, power, campuses) hold in our scenarios A and B, bend in C, and break only in D/E — which is precisely the confidence ordering both pages assign.
